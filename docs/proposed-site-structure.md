# Work Solutions: proposed website structure and content brief

Status: proposal for discussion, 24 September 2026. This document proposes the information architecture and content;
it does not authorise or specify website implementation.

Based on [the growth plan](growth-plan.md), [the initial review](review.md), [the case-study material](case-studies.md),
and the subsequent clarification that the projects are real and photographs are available.

## 1. Purpose and positioning

The website should attract profitable, multi-day electrical projects and repeat business relationships with contractors,
developers, landlords, property managers, and commercial owners. A full residential rewire remains relevant, as does a
smaller visit within an ongoing portfolio relationship. Project value and relationship potential guide the positioning.

Electrical contracting leads the proposition. Plumbing, gas, and carpentry support relevant refurbishment case studies
and can be mentioned as additional capabilities where their availability and delivery arrangements are confirmed.

Proposed homepage headline:

> Electrical installations and ongoing maintenance for contractors, landlords and businesses.

Proposed supporting copy:

> Commercial installations, rewires, HMO electrical packages, fire alarms and emergency lighting across Somerset and
> the Bristol area. Discuss an upcoming project or electrical maintenance across your properties.

Geographical wording is provisional: confirm the preferred operating area, including whether Bath should be explicit.
The copy is a direction for discussion; credentials, service scope, and delivery promises need factual confirmation.

## 2. Why a multipage site fits

Separate pages let a contractor assess project delivery, a landlord explore HMO work, and a property manager investigate
ongoing maintenance without searching through one long page. They also provide useful destinations for introductions,
tender conversations, and project references.

Each page must answer a distinct buyer question and contain specific evidence. The initial site should remain easy to
maintain, with shared page layouts and a curated project collection. Additional pages should follow substantive content.

## 3. Navigation and sitemap

Main navigation:

**Services | Maintenance | Projects | About | Contact**

The logo links to Home. A prominent **Discuss your project** button links to the contact page. Telephone and email remain
easy to find. Services opens an accessible list of service pages and also has its own overview page.

```text
Home                                      /
Services                                  /services/
  Commercial Electrical                   /services/commercial-electrical/
  Rewires & Refurbishments                 /services/rewires-refurbishments/
  HMO Electrical Packages                 /services/hmo-electrical/
```

This gives ten core pages, plus individual case studies and supporting policy content. Maintenance appears prominently
in the main navigation because repeat business is a central objective. It is also linked from the Services overview.

At launch, audience-specific sections within these pages can address contractors, developers, landlords, and commercial
owners. Separate sector pages can follow when there is enough distinct evidence and content to justify them.

## 4. Page briefs

### Home

**Buyer question:** Is this a suitable electrical contractor for my project or property portfolio?

Recommended order:

1. Clear headline, service area, strong photograph from a relevant project, and **Discuss your project** action.
2. Concise credentials and evidence: verified contractor status and an accurately described experience statement.
3. Two clear routes: **Electrical projects** and **Ongoing maintenance**, each linking to the relevant page.
4. Three featured case studies, with scope, property type, and a meaningful fact about delivery.
5. Short service summaries linking to the detailed service pages.
6. How Work Solutions works with contractors, developers, property managers, and commercial owners.
7. A concise account of enquiry, scope review, delivery, and handover, reflecting the actual business process.
8. An attributed business-client testimonial and a final enquiry invitation.

Show the director and team briefly, with a link to About. Use selected project photographs with meaningful captions.
Avoid allowing a large gallery to dominate the page or obscure the commercial proposition.

### Services overview

**Buyer question:** Which service fits the work I need?

Provide short, concrete summaries of the four project services and the maintenance offer. Each summary should identify
typical work, relevant buyers, and a link to details. Briefly explain how several services can form one electrical package.

Use this page as a useful orientation point for mixed enquiries. Detailed scope and evidence belong on the service pages.

### Commercial Electrical

**Buyer question:** Can this company deliver the electrical installation or upgrade my premises or project needs?

Cover commercial installations, distribution equipment upgrades, power and lighting, and work alongside other contractors,
using only confirmed capabilities. Explain how scope, access, planned interruptions, coordination, testing, and handover
are handled. Distinguish installation, design, and commissioning responsibilities accurately.

Feature the commercial distribution upgrade and a relevant contractor-led project. Link refurbishment enquiries to the
rewires page and ongoing requirements to Maintenance. Primary action: **Discuss a commercial project**.

### Rewires & Refurbishments

**Buyer question:** Can Work Solutions deliver the electrical package for my rewire, conversion, or refurbishment?

Cover full and partial rewires, upgrades, and electrical work within development and refurbishment projects. Address
coordination, phasing, access, and completion requirements where supported by actual experience. Include substantial
residential projects alongside commercial work.

Use the full-property rewire and a refurbishment case study. Explain any additional trade coordination through an actual
example, with clear responsibility. Primary action: **Discuss a rewire or refurbishment**.

### HMO Electrical Packages

**Buyer question:** Can one electrical contractor coordinate the installation work my HMO conversion needs?

Explain the electrical scope available: circuits, distribution, detection, emergency lighting, testing, and handover,
as applicable to the specific property and agreed requirements. Describe the relationship with the developer, landlord,
and other project participants.

Feature the completed HMO project prominently. Avoid presenting electrical work as a guarantee of whole-property HMO
approval. Link to Maintenance for subsequent portfolio work. Primary action: **Discuss an HMO project**.

### Fire Alarms & Emergency Lighting

**Buyer question:** Can Work Solutions install or upgrade these systems and support them afterwards?

Describe confirmed installation, alteration, commissioning, and servicing capabilities. Explain the enquiry information
needed to establish scope, and identify what records or handover information are provided. Keep distinctions between
different systems and responsibilities clear.

Use the fire alarm and emergency lighting case study. Link ongoing testing and maintenance to the Maintenance page.
Primary action: **Discuss your systems**.

Start with one combined page because the draft material includes a combined project. Separate pages can follow if each
service develops enough distinct content and demand.

### Maintenance & Landlord Compliance

**Buyer question:** Can this company become our regular electrical contractor across one or several properties?

This page owns the ongoing relationship proposition. Cover confirmed offerings such as EICR programmes, remedial works,
planned electrical maintenance, emergency lighting testing, and fire alarm maintenance.

Explain practical arrangements: agreeing the property list and scope, access coordination, scheduling, records, reporting,
and follow-up work. Describe actual reporting and communication methods; do not imply a customer portal or guaranteed
response service unless those exist.

Use evidence of repeat appointments or portfolio work, including the number of sites and length of relationship where
available. Link to project pages when major upgrades are required. Primary action: **Discuss ongoing maintenance**.

### Projects

**Buyer question:** Have they delivered work relevant to mine?

Use a simple, curated grid. Each project card includes a representative photograph, informative title, building or client
type, location where publishable, and a short scope summary. Link every card to a dedicated case-study page.

The initial collection should represent HMO work, a full rewire, a commercial distribution upgrade, fire alarms and
emergency lighting, and a substantial contractor-led or repeat-client project. The existing EV and ventilation projects
can also be included with appropriate context. Prioritise the strongest evidence in the first positions.

A small collection needs no search interface or elaborate filtering. Project labels and clear titles provide orientation.

### About & Credentials

**Buyer question:** Who will I be working with, and what supports their credibility?

Include the director, team roles, relevant experience, operating area, and how work is supervised and coordinated.
Explain the business's approach to ongoing client relationships with concrete examples.

Present the exact verified NICEIC status and a verification link. Confirm current SMAS status before including it.
State other relevant credentials and insurance information accurately, with further details available on request where
appropriate. Distinguish company history from individuals' years of experience.

Explain additional trades here where useful, while retaining electrical contracting as the site's main offer. Use the
correct legal company identity consistently, including its relationship to the Work Solutions trading name.

### Contact

**Buyer question:** How do I get the right person to assess my project or maintenance requirement?

Suggested short form:

| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Name | Required |
| Email | Required |
| Company or organisation | Optional, since substantial homeowner projects remain relevant |
| Telephone | Optional |
| Enquiry type | Project work, ongoing maintenance, or other |
| Project/site location | Required; town or postcode is sufficient initially |
| Work required | Required free text |
| Preferred timing | Optional |

Display an email route for drawings, specifications, or tender documents. An upload feature can be considered if email
proves insufficient. A brief confirmation should describe the real next step without inventing a response-time promise.

For maintenance enquiries, invite visitors to include the number and type of properties in their description. Project
enquiries should invite scope and intended dates. Keep telephone contact available and link the privacy information.

## 5. Shared service-page structure

Use a consistent layout that keeps production and maintenance straightforward:

1. Service proposition and relevant project photograph.
2. Typical work and who it is for.
3. Scope and delivery arrangements.
4. Relevant completed project or projects.
5. Applicable credentials and an attributed testimonial where available.
6. A few genuine buyer questions, only where useful.
7. Specific enquiry action and links to related services.

Each page should have unique, useful content. Repeated generic assurances about quality will not establish capability.

## 6. Case-study content brief

All proposed case-study subjects have been confirmed as completed work with photographs available. The next editorial
step is to connect each outline with its specific facts and images.

| Element | Information to collect |
|---|---|
| Title | Work delivered and building type, with location where useful |
| Project facts | Location, completion date, duration, approximate scale |
| Appointment | Who commissioned Work Solutions; distinguish contractor, end client, and site owner |
| Brief | What the customer needed and why |
| Scope | Exactly what Work Solutions delivered and coordinated |
| Constraints | Access, occupation, phasing, deadlines, or coordination challenges actually encountered |
| Delivery | How the team addressed those constraints |
| Outcome | Specific completed work, testing, handover, and evidenced results |
| Photographs | One lead image and a small supporting selection with factual captions |
| Reference | Approved client quotation or reference, if available |
| Next step | Link to the relevant service and a similar-project enquiry |

Project value can help communicate scale if the client is comfortable publishing it. Duration, property count, areas
covered, or installation quantities can provide useful context without disclosing price.

Obtain agreement for published client names, logos, quotations, and identifiable project material. Contractor-led public
sector experience should state the appointment chain clearly. A portfolio name alone should not imply a direct contract.

## 7. Scope boundaries and later additions

- Keep EV charging visible through its case study and relevant service content. A dedicated page should follow confirmation
  of the commercial or multi-charger offer the company wants to promote; one charger project does not establish fleet scale.
- Use BMS, heating controls, solar, and public-sector experience in accurately scoped case studies. Dedicated service pages
  depend on confirming what Work Solutions offers directly and wants to sell going forward.
- Include plumbing, gas, and carpentry where they strengthen a real project example. Standalone pages would broaden the
  proposition and should be a deliberate business decision.
- Add sector pages only when they answer distinct questions and can draw on relevant completed work.
- A regularly updated blog is optional. Strong service and project pages are the initial content priority.

## 8. Design and delivery principles

Retain the recognisable black-and-yellow identity as the starting point. Give real project photography, readable text,
and clear navigation priority. Use visible links to case studies and credentials so buyers can assess evidence quickly.

Use a limited set of reusable page layouts, appropriately sized images, and restrained scripts and animation. The
multipage structure should not multiply unnecessary assets or maintenance effort. Mobile navigation, keyboard access,
form labels, contrast, and meaningful image descriptions belong in the eventual implementation brief.

Each service and project page needs a descriptive title, a meaningful page heading, a concise search description, and
relevant internal links. Confirm the operating area and describe it consistently. Avoid creating near-identical town pages.

This proposal is platform-independent. A decision on retaining Squarespace or changing platforms should follow agreement
on content, editing needs, and design requirements.

## 9. Review decisions

Please review the proposed navigation, service grouping, and prominence of Maintenance first. These decisions determine
the main journeys and can be agreed before full copy or visual design begins.

Before final copy, confirm the service area, current credentials, maintenance arrangements, and the initial project facts
and photographs. These are content preparation tasks; they do not prevent review of this proposed structure.

The intended measures of success are qualified multi-day project enquiries, suitable maintenance and portfolio enquiries,
new repeat-client relationships, and the value and profitability of resulting work. Enquiry volume alone is insufficient.
