The website has useful foundations, but it does not yet make a convincing case for larger commercial contracts. It establishes that Work Solutions offers several trades and has satisfied customers. It gives much less evidence about the company's ability to manage scope, resources, deadlines, and delivery on a substantial project.

I reviewed the live website, the saved HTML and assets (current/work-solutions.html), and the live sitemap. This is a content and source review, with inspection of some image assets—not a full browser-based desktop/mobile or performance audit. No files were changed.

The most important findings are:

| Area | Finding | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Commercial ambitions are mixed with domestic service messaging | Buyers receive an unclear picture of the work the company specialises in |
| Commercial credibility | Relevant projects appear in a gallery with little explanation | The strongest evidence is underused |
| Scope of service | The site primarily presents electricians and plumbers, with carpentry added later | It does not clearly establish a broader building contractor proposition |
| Enquiries | Contact details and a form exist, but the journey is generic | Commercial buyers get little guidance on discussing a project or inviting a tender |
| Content quality | Generic claims, small-job testimonials, and editing errors | These weaken the impression of care and capability |

## The first decision is what kind of contractor he wants buyers to see.

You describe a building company, but the opening message primarily offers electrical and plumbing professionals. Carpentry appears further down. There is no clear explanation of whether Work Solutions takes responsibility for an entire refurbishment, supplies individual trade packages, or provides ongoing building maintenance.

Those are different offers with different buyers:

- Main contractors need dependable subcontractors for defined work packages.
- Commercial owners and occupiers may want one contractor to manage a refurbishment.
- Property and facilities managers may want maintenance, repeat work, and reliable coverage.

The website currently leaves visitors to work this out. Before redesigning it, I would establish which of these represents his actual capability and preferred business.

## The commercial opening is contradicted by much of the supporting material.

The site already addresses major commercial projects around Bristol, Bath, and Somerset. However, the services and testimonials give substantial attention to household-scale work. One testimonial describes fitting two lights; carpentry examples include wardrobes and bookshelves. These observations are visible on the current homepage.

There is nothing wrong with doing that work. The issue is prominence: the evidence visitors encounter should support the type of contract he wants next.

Commercial messaging would be stronger if it explained concrete capabilities: the packages delivered, environments worked in, responsibility taken, and how projects are organised. Broad assurances about quality do little to distinguish him from other firms.

## The project gallery is probably the biggest missed opportunity.

The saved site contains references to office refurbishment, student accommodation, floodlighting, and work involving GSH Projects and Unite Students. These are promising starting points, but photographs alone do not establish the extent of Work Solutions' contribution.

For example, a photograph of a completed office does not tell a buyer whether the company refurbished the whole space or installed its lighting.

I would prioritise three or four substantial case studies, each explaining:

- The client or main contractor, with permission to name them.
- The building type, location, and brief.
- Work Solutions' precise scope and contractual role.
- Project scale, duration, and practical constraints.
- The result, supported by photographs and ideally a client reference.

Where work was performed through another contractor, that relationship should be explicit. Accurate subcontracting experience is valuable evidence in its own right.

## The site needs to answer more of a commercial buyer's practical questions.

The team section is a positive feature: named people make the business tangible and accountable. However, job titles alone do not explain how a larger contract would be delivered.

I could not find clear explanations of project supervision, resource capacity, scheduling, coordination with other trades, quality checks, or handover. Their absence from the website does not mean the company lacks them; it means a buyer cannot assess them.

Similarly, the saved page includes a SMAS logo and mentions trade credentials, but these are not developed into an easily understood account of current qualifications and their scope. Any revised presentation should use verified details and give buyers a way to check them.

A small company can make a strong commercial proposition through director involvement, clear responsibility, and proven delivery. It does not need to imply that it is larger than it is.

## The enquiry route works as a general contact mechanism, but could better support commercial work.

The saved page has clickable phone and email links, plus a form collecting names, email, subject, and message. Those are useful foundations.

However:

- The prominent contact button initiates a telephone call; its wording could make that clearer.
- There is no explicit invitation to send a specification or tender enquiry.
- The form does not prompt for company, project location, scope, or intended timing.
- The newsletter option competes with the immediate purpose of a project enquiry.

A short commercial enquiry form could gather the essentials without demanding a lengthy brief. An accompanying email route could explain how to send drawings or tender documents. Any response-time promise should reflect what the business can consistently deliver.

## The wider presentation needs more precision and stronger editorial control.

Several details in the saved copy deserve attention:

- The carpentry section ends with a reference to electrical work, apparently copied from another section.
- Capitalisation and writing style are inconsistent.
- Many gallery image descriptions are camera filenames or WhatsApp filenames.
- Testimonials lack names, dates, or project context alongside the quotations.
- The footer still displays 2024.
- The public branding and terms-document filename use different company-name forms; their relationship should be clear.

Individually, these are small issues. Together, they make the site feel less carefully maintained.

The black-and-yellow branding is distinctive and recognisably associated with construction. I would not assume that a new identity is necessary. Based on the assets, the greater opportunity is to use that branding consistently around strong project photography and clear information. Actual spacing, mobile presentation, and visual hierarchy still need inspection in a rendered browser.

## There are also specific technical improvements to make.

These findings come principally from the saved source:

| Finding | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Page title is only the company name | It gives little context about services or location |
| Meta description is empty | An opportunity to explain the business in search results is unused |
| Five section-level H1 headings; opening proposition is paragraph text | The heading structure could communicate the page's purpose more clearly |
| Many project images have filename-based alternative text | Those descriptions are unhelpful to people using screen readers |
| Some image-only links have empty alternative text | Their accessible names need checking |
| No privacy-policy link found in the inspected page | This deserves attention alongside the enquiry form and newsletter option |
| Saved assets total roughly 19 MB, including roughly 11 MB of images | There is enough weight to justify a measured performance review |

The saved asset total is not the live initial download size. Compression, caching, responsive images, and lazy loading affect actual loading behaviour. Some lazy loading is already present, so I would not label the site slow without measurements.

The live sitemap lists only the home page. This gives individual services and projects little opportunity to act as focused search destinations or useful links in sales conversations. A modest set of substantive service and case-study pages would have a clear purpose.

Nothing found so far establishes a need to replace Squarespace. Most of the important weaknesses concern content, evidence, and structure.

My priority would be to agree the commercial offer, then build the website around evidence of it. The highest-value material is likely already within the business: completed projects, repeat clients, delivery experience, and the director's knowledge of how work is managed.

Before proposing changes, I would want to clarify four things:

1. What does "bigger" mean in approximate contract value and type of work?
2. Does he want whole building/refurbishment contracts, specialist trade packages, maintenance agreements, or a particular combination?
3. Who should appoint him: main contractors, property managers, commercial owners, or another group?
4. Which completed projects best demonstrate that capability, and can those clients provide references?

Those answers would let us assess the site against a specific market and develop a focused proposal for your feedback.
